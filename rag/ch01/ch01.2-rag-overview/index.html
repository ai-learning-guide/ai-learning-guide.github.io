In the previous section, we introduced in detail the background and motivation for the emergence of RAG, understood why RAG is needed, and what RAG can ultimately help us achieve. In this section, we will introduce the basic process of RAG development.

1. Core Motivation: Why is RAG needed?#

First, let’s do a simple review.

The primary driving force behind RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) lies in the limitations of LLM pre-trained data. Despite the large scale of LLM pre-training, they cannot incorporate users’private dataorthe latest dataafter pre-training.

Therefore, we can regard LLM as a"kernel" of a new type of operating system, and connecting this kernel to external data is the core capability for the development of this system. Meanwhile, as the context window of LLM continues to expand (from thousands of tokens to a scale that can accommodate hundreds of pages of information), directly providing external information to the model through RAG has become a feasible and efficient task.

2. The Three Basic Stages of RAG#

Overall, the general paradigm of RAG typically consists of the following three key steps:

  • Indexing: Preprocess and store external documents for subsequent retrieval based on queries.
  • Retrieval: In response to the user’s question, search for the most relevant segments from the stored documents.
  • Generation : Input the retrieved relevant documents along with the user’s question into the LLM to generate a grounded answer.

Further, for the entire lifecycle of RAG, it includes

  • Document Processing: Load external documents (such as blog posts) and Splitting them into Chunks of 1000 characters in size.
  • Vectorized Storage: For example, OpenAI’s embedding model can be used to convert chunks into vectors and store them in a local Chroma or Milvus vector database.
  • Logic Chain (Chain) Construction: Define a process that automatically runs a retriever to obtain relevant documents, fills them into a predefined prompt template (e.g., “You are a Q&A assistant… Use the following retrieved content to answer the question…”), and finally hands it over to the LLM to generate an answer.

3. Tools and Observability#

In the entire process of building the RAG pipeline, to ensure the transparency of the system, we will subsequently use the LangSmith component developed by LangChain for tracing and observability analysis. Through LangSmith, developers can clearly see which specific documents the retriever has extracted, as well as the complete content of the prompt ultimately passed to the LLM, which is crucial for debugging and optimizing the pipeline.
