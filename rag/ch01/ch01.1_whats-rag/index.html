Ch1.1 What's RAG?

March 21, 2026 · WangCheng
Table of Contents

Large Language Models (LLMs) are trained on large but fixed corpora, which limits their ability to reason about private or up-to-date information. Fine-tuning is a way to mitigate this issue, but it is generally not suitable for recalling factual information and is relatively costly. Against this backdrop, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has gradually emerged as a mainstream and powerful mechanism. RAG uses documents retrieved from external data sources as a Knowledge Base to enhance the generation capabilities of LLMs through in-context learning.

1. Why is it not enough to rely solely on large models?#

When many people first integrate large models into their business, they often have an illusion:

“The model is so powerful that as long as the question is clearly asked, it should be able to answer anything, right?”

But the reality is often that it (the model) answers confidently but is often wrong, which is what people commonly refer to as talking nonsense in a serious manner.

For example, if you ask the large model: What is the travel reimbursement standard for XXX Company in 2024?

At this point, the model will answer based on its own inherent knowledge, and the answers usually have three characteristics: ① The language is very professional; ② The logic is very complete; ③ But the content is completely wrong.

At this point, many people’s first reaction is: Is the prompt not well-written enough? Is the model not large enough? Should we try a few more times?

But the problem actually lies neither with you nor with the model, but rather with a very important yet often overlooked fact:

Large models are neither databases nor search engines.

You can understand it as: a person who has read a large number of books, but when answering questions, cannot refer to books on the spot and can only rely on “the most similar content in memory” to respond.

This will bring three natural limitations:

① Knowledge is static: Once the model is trained, its knowledge is frozen, and it has no idea that your company just updated a version of the regulations yesterday.

② We don’t know your private data: internal company documents, business rules, and technical detail models were never seen during training, so naturally we cannot answer accurately.

③ will generate “plausible-sounding wrong answers” : because its goal is to “generate the most likely text” rather than “return existing materials”.

So, this is also why the hallucinations of large models are not bugs but structural issues.

2. Can fine-tuning be used to solve it?#

Many people would think of a seemingly reasonable solution at this point: “Why not just take the company’s data to fine-tune the model?”

It sounds right, but in most knowledge-based scenarios, it is not appropriate.

The reason is simple: fine-tuning does not enable the model to “learn to consult documents”; it merely makes the model more inclined towards a certain response style. Moreover, and more crucially, once the data changes, retraining is required, which is not only costly but also time-consuming, making it unsuitable for high-frequency changing content such as regulations, documents, and FAQs.

To summarize in one sentence:Fine-tuning addresses “how to answer”, but cannot address “based on what materials to answer”.

3. How did RAG come about?#

By now, our requirements are actually very clear: we don’t want the model to “memorize all content”, but rather we hope it - to check the real materials before answering.

This is exactly the core idea of RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation).

In a nutshell, RAG = first retrieve relevant information, then let the large model generate answers. You can think of it as a highly humanized process: for example, a new customer service representative will first check the Knowledge Base before answering questions, and a technical support engineer will first review documents before solving problems, and finally organize the answers in their own words.

What RAG does is essentially to transfer this process to the large model. As shown in Figure 1, this is the core process of RAG when handling problems.

4. What “real-world problems” does RAG truly solve?#

Once RAG is introduced, the role of the large model changes. At this point, the large model no longer answers based on “memory” butanswers based on real documents.

That’s why it has truly become usable in many scenarios, such as enterprise internal system/process Q&A, technical documentation, API documentation assistant, private Knowledge Base + intelligent Q&A system, etc.

Of course, what’s even more important is that the content of the answers can be traced back to its source, so that when there are errors, it can be located which document the problem originated from, thereby reducing the occurrence of “model hallucinations”.

Therefore, the value of RAG can be summarized in one sentence: RAG enables large models to truly speak based on your data for the first time.
