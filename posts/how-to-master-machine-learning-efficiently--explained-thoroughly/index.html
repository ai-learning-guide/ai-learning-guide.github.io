How to Master Machine Learning Efficiently — Explained Thoroughly

March 23, 2026 · WangCheng
Table of Contents

For beginners, “How can I get started with Machine Learning?” and “Should I start with theory or practice when getting started with Machine Learning?” are all well-worn topics.

Thinking back to when I first started learning Machine Learning, wasn’t it the same? I always felt like I was just lingering outside, unable to find the right way in. Fortunately, after a long period of exploration, I’ve also summarized a learning path that suits me, and I’ll share it with you all next.

1. What is a good method?#

I believe that the most crucial thing in learning Machine Learning well is not to rush to choose various learning materials or videos, but to first understand how to effectively get started with Machine Learning from a methodological perspective. A good method yields twice the result with half the effort, while a poor method yields half the result with twice the effort. Of course, in essence there is no such thing as a good or bad method, only a matter of suitability; a method that suits oneself can be called a good method . Therefore, this also answers questions such as “Why can’t I understand things like Li Hang’s Statistical Learning Methods or Andrew Ng’s videos about Machine Learning?”

No book can possibly make everyone think it’s “good”. This “good” doesn’t refer to evaluating its quality, but rather whether it suits oneself. What suits oneself is considered “good”, while what doesn’t is “garbage”. Meanwhile, no book can basically cover every knowledge point well. For you, perhaps only one algorithm in a certain book is well explained and particularly suitable for you, but you can’t understand the rest. Maybe others only need one book to learn 10 algorithms, while you need 10 books, but even in such a situation, I think it’s worth it.

So, it’s okay if you don’t understand the materials you currently have in hand, but you must never give up looking for a book or video that suits you. It’s true that there is a certain threshold when first starting out in Machine Learning, so don’t give up easily. At the same time, it’s especially important to note the reward feedback mechanism. Choose a step-by-step, progressive learning method so that you can feel a sense of gain, achievement, and joy every day or half-day while learning an algorithm. Only in this way can you have stronger motivation to continue learning and form a positive feedback loop.

2. How to learn?#

When I first systematically studied Machine Learning, the materials I came across were Mr. Wu Enda’s Machine Learning video courses. It has to be said that Mr. Wu Enda’s course content is indeed easy to understand, and most of the content is also explained in great detail. For beginners, the course content is definitely full of real stuff. However, as I continued learning, I gradually found that this course didn’t seem to be a good fit for me. Since I hadn’t found better learning materials at that time, I had no choice but to grit my teeth and continue watching. It wasn’t until I picked up Mr. Li Hang’s textbook on Statistical Machine Learning for the second time to continue my studies that I finally summarized a learning path suitable for myself. To sum it up in one sentence: First grasp the main points, then tackle the details .

Never underestimate these eight words. Many friends may fail to get started with Machine Learning precisely because no one has told them this truth.

Learning an algorithm is like traversing all the branches and nodes of a large tree. The more complex the algorithm, the more lush its branches and leaves. Generally speaking, there are two ways to traverse this tree: Depth-First Traversal and Trunk-First Traversal. For some people, the first method may be suitable: starting from the root at the bottom, whenever reaching a branch, they traverse it deeply, then return to the trunk to continue traversing the second branch until the traversal is completed; while for others, the second method may be more suitable: starting from the root at the bottom, they climb along the trunk to the top of the tree to first gain a concept of the overall structure of the tree, and then start from the root to traverse the entire tree like the first method.Compared to the first method, the second method is less likely to “get lost” during the traversal process because we first gain an understanding of the overall structure of the tree at the beginning.

Therefore, for the study of an algorithm, I have summarized it into 5 levels (3 stages):

Phase 1 can be seen as the process of first climbing from the main trunk of a tree to the top to get a glimpse of the whole tree, because for an algorithm, the most fundamental thing is the idea behind it, which is also the soul of an algorithm; Phase 2 and Phase 3 can be seen as the levels after traversing the entire tree, which are specific explorations of the details.

So why is it in the above order? Can it be shuffled? My answer is: Of course it can,as long as it is a method suitable for oneself, it is a good method. However, I still strongly recommend following the above order for learning.Regrettably, the learning order of most people (or materials) nowadays is either ①②④⑤③ or ①②④③⑤. The drawback of these two learning orders is that many algorithms are difficult in mathematical derivation (such as support vector machines), and when people cannot overcome this difficulty, they will not continue. The final result is thatthey neither thoroughly understand the principle nor learn how to use it. On the contrary, what I have always advocated is: First learn how to use it, then explore why.

Meanwhile, some may ask: ① Is it okay to skip directly to ③ after learning? My answer is: Absolutely not! Because this will turn you into a complete parameter-tuning enthusiast, with all parameter combinations blindly tried out in your eyes, and you won’t understand the specific meaning behind each parameter (what happens if it’s too large, what happens if it’s too small), even though you can claim to know how to use open-source frameworks. The specific corresponding learning steps and stage divisions will be discussed in detail in the learning materials I provide at the end of the article.

3. When should I stop learning?#

Another issue faced by beginners is determining the appropriate level of mastery for an algorithm. It’s like when someone asks: For a research paper, should I just understand the principles or strive to implement it as much as possible? One can imagine that if the learning process of an algorithm hadn’t been previously summarized into the three stages mentioned above, I really wouldn’t know how to tell you where you should aim to reach in your learning at this moment.

Here, my suggestion is that for all algorithms, stage one must be completed; for some basic or relatively easy algorithms (such as linear regression, Logistic Regression, etc.), one can require oneself to reach the above three stages; for those more difficult algorithms (such as SVM, decision tree, etc.), one can require oneself to achieve only the first two stages. At the same time, it is necessary to clearly recognize that for the learning of any algorithm, only a very small number of people can reach the state of fully understanding it after just one study, so one should not hold the idea that learning once is enough. For example, reach stage one in the first study, stage two in the second study, and so on. This phased learning approach can relatively easily enable oneself to gain a sense of satisfaction and enjoy the pleasure of learning. Meanwhile, after learning about 3 - 4 algorithms following the above steps, one can be considered to have taken the first step into Machine Learning!

4. Summary#

For the entire content, I will summarize it in 4 sentences here:

① There is no such thing as a good or bad method; there is only a difference between suitability and unsuitability. A method that suits oneself can be called a good method;

② Choose a step-by-step, progressive learning method, and make sure to form a closed loop of positive feedback in your learning process;

③ During the learning process, one should learn to grasp the main points first and then the details, without putting the cart before the horse;

④ Follow the learning principle of first learning how to use, then exploring why.
