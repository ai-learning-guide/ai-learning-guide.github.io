What are soft label? How do they differ from hard label?

April 1, 2026 · WangCheng

  • 2. What is a soft label?

  • 4. Why are soft labels more effective in distillation?
  • 5. Summary

In Model Training (especially model distillation) and Deep learning, Hard Targets/Labels and Soft Targets/Labels are two different ways to describe sample category information. Their core difference lies in the dimension of the information they contain and their impact on model training.

1. What is a hard label?#

Hard labels are labels in the form of One-hot Encoding provided by the original dataset.

  • Manifestation: It only indicates the single correct category to which the sample belongs. For example, in a three-class classification task, if the sample belongs to the third class, its hard label is [0,0,1].
  • Information Content : The information is very singular. It can only tell the model “which category this sample belongs to” without providing any additional information about the correlation between categories.

2. What is a soft label?#

Soft labels typically refer to the probability distribution output by a trained “teacher model”.

  • Representation : It is a probability vector with values across all categories. For example, the soft label output by the teacher model for the same sample might be $[0.35, 0.28, 0.37]$.
  • Generation Method: In model distillation, smoother soft labels are typically generated by increasing the temperature of the Softmax function ($T$), with the formula $$q_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}.$$
  • Information Content : Contains extremely rich information and can reveal Inter-class Correlation .

4. Why are soft labels more effective in distillation?#

Because soft labels not only indicate the correct answer but also reflect the model’sinferential bias or uncertaintywhen processing new data.

If the probability of a sample belonging to the correct category is high (e.g., 0.9), it indicates that the model has strong discriminative ability and is confident; if the probability distribution is relatively uniform (e.g., $[0.35, 0.28, 0.37]$), it reflects that the sample has high ambiguity or the model has weak discriminative ability on this sample.

These subtle probability differences reflect how the model generalizes to unknown samples. By mimicking this distribution, the student model can acquire generalization ability similar to that of the complex model.

For example, for the following example, the model can be used to complete the prediction of the [MASK] part.

I think this is the beginning of a beautiful [MASK].

Based on the understanding of the context of this sentence, the model may assign high probabilities to the two words “day” and “life”, but at the same time, it will also assign lower probabilities to words such as “future”, “story”, “world”… (referred to as long-tail words). The former indicates that these two words are highly appropriate in this context, and the model has high confidence in them; the latter indicates that although the probabilities of these words are low, they are still suitable candidate words and can also make the sentence reasonable in certain contexts.

Simply put, if the sum of the probabilities assigned by the model to the words “day” and “life” is 1, and the probabilities of all other words are 0, then we can reasonably suspect that the model has merely memorized the situations that appear in the training set, without any generalization ability. Therefore, the difference between high-probability and low-probability words means that the model is not just “memorizing” the fixed patterns in the training data, but is capable of understanding the richness and diversity of language. This ability enables it to make flexible predictions when faced with unseen text, rather than simply relying too much on some obvious answers.

5. Summary#

Hard labels are “black-and-white” conclusions, while soft labels are probability descriptions that “contain logic”. In model distillation, the student model learns not only the correct answers but also the generalization logic of the teacher model when processing new data by imitating the soft labels output by the teacher model.
